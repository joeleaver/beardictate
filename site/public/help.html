First run

The first time you start beardictate, it downloads the models it needs: a speech model of about 640 MB, then a cleanup model of 0.6 to 2.5 GB. It picks the cleanup model by trying it on your computer, so a fast machine gets a bigger model and a laptop gets one that keeps up. Every file is checked against a known checksum, and an interrupted download resumes where it stopped.

You can start dictating as soon as the speech model is in. Until the cleanup model arrives, you get your words with punctuation but without the tidying.

beardictate lives in your system tray as a small bear. Its colour shows what it’s doing: loading, listening, cleaning up, or asleep. On Windows, new tray icons hide in the overflow menu, so drag the bear onto the taskbar where you can see it.

Starting and stopping

Put your cursor where you want the text, in any app, and talk. beardictate types each sentence a moment after you finish it, while you’re already saying the next.

Wake and sleep by voice

The microphone stays on while beardictate runs. Say bear, wake up to start typing and bear, go to sleep to stop. While it’s asleep, nothing you say is typed or kept. Turn the microphone off completely from the tray menu.

The shortcut

Press Ctrl+` (the key left of 1) to wake or sleep it. You can change the shortcut in Settings. It must use at least two keys, so you can’t set it off by accident.

If you’d rather the microphone were only on while you dictate, turn off Always listening in Settings. The shortcut then switches the microphone and typing on and off together.

Voice commands

Say these as part of what you’re dictating. They’re recognised by fixed rules, so they always work the same way, and phrases like the new paragraph in your prose are left as words.

SayWhat happens
new paragraphStarts a new paragraph.
new lineStarts a new line.
open quote … close quotePuts what’s between them in quotation marks. Also quote … end quote, unquote.
scratch thatRemoves the last sentence. If it’s already been typed, it’s erased. Say it again to go back further.
bear, wake upStarts typing what you say.
bear, go to sleepStops typing. What you said before it is still typed.

You usually don’t need the quote commands: beardictate recognises dialog and adds the quotes for you. Use them when it guesses wrong or when you want to be sure.

Special words

Fantasy names, invented places, a character called Ysolde: speech recognition has never heard them. Add them under Special words in Settings, with any ways it tends to mishear them:

Ysolde: Isolde, Ussel

beardictate listens harder for those words and corrects the mishearings to your spelling. Keep a group of words per book and switch groups on and off as you move between projects.

Settings worth knowing

Paragraphs

By default a new paragraph starts where you say new paragraph and when a different character starts speaking. You can also have one start when you pause for a few seconds, and choose between a blank line or a single return between paragraphs.

Quotation marks

Double or single quotes, curly or straight, and US placement (To the river, he said) or UK placement, where punctuation goes inside the quotes only when it belongs to the speech. Match whatever your manuscript uses.

Cleanup model

Pick a smaller model if typing feels slow, or a larger one for better dialog. Off types exactly what the speech model heard, with only your voice commands applied.

System requirements

WindowsWindows 10 or 11, 64-bit. The installer is coming soon.
Linux64-bit x86. KDE Plasma or GNOME on Wayland, or X11.
macOSPlanned after Windows.
Memory8 GB is enough. With 16 GB or more it can use a larger cleanup model.
GraphicsNot needed. A graphics card (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel, through Vulkan) makes cleanup faster.
DiskAbout 640 MB for the speech model, plus 0.6 to 2.5 GB for the cleanup model.
MicrophoneAny. A headset mic gives the cleanest results in a noisy room.

Questions

Does anything I say get sent anywhere?
While beardictate is an alpha, yes: every copy sends its developer the recordings of what you dictate, the text recognized from them, what the cleanup changed and what was typed, with timings and your computer’s details, tied to a random install number rather than your name. Only what you say while dictating is sent; with “Always listening” on, nothing said while it’s asleep leaves your computer. Speech recognition and cleanup themselves run on your computer. The details.
Will it change what I wrote?
Only in the ways you’d want: punctuation, capitals, quotes, paragraphs, and removing ums and repeated words. Every sentence is compared with what you said, and if the cleanup changed any other word, you get your own words instead.
Which apps does it work in?
Anything you can type into: Word, Scrivener, Google Docs in a browser, Obsidian, a plain text editor.
Why does it need such a big download?
The models that understand speech and tidy prose are what let it work without the cloud. They download once, and the app itself stays small.
Windows warns me the installer is from an unknown publisher.
Early builds may not be code-signed yet. Check the file’s SHA-256 against the one on the download page, then choose More info and Run anyway.
Does it work in languages other than English?
The speech model understands 25 European languages, but the cleanup and voice commands are built for English fiction for now.
Is it really free?
Yes. It’s open source under the MIT license, and the models it uses are openly licensed.
