Talk your story.
It lands as prose.

beardictate types what you say into whatever you write in, already tidied: the ums and false starts gone, punctuation and capitals in place, and, if you write fiction, dialog in quotes with a new paragraph when someone else speaks. It runs on your own computer, so nothing you say leaves it.

It tidies as you talk, and leaves your words alone

Speak the way you would to a friend. Your words appear where your cursor is as you say them, in Word, Scrivener, Google Docs, a plain text file, anything, and when you pause, beardictate tidies the sentence in place.

  • Dialog goes in quotes

    Writing fiction? Turn on Dialog and you don’t say “open quote”: it hears which words are speech and quotes them, with the commas and capitals in the right places and a new paragraph when the speaker changes.

    she said we're leaving now and he said fine go
    She said, “We’re leaving now.”
    And he said, “Fine. Go.”

  • The ums disappear

    Fillers, stutters and false starts come out. Nothing else does.

    the the uh the lighthouse was um dark
    The lighthouse was dark.

  • Commands when you want them

    Start a paragraph where you choose, or take back the last sentence. You can still say “open quote” if it ever guesses wrong.

    it was raining new paragraph it was snowing scratch that
    It was raining.“It was snowing” is erased, and a new paragraph waits for what you say next.

  • Your characters’ names

    Teach it the names and invented words of your book, and how it tends to mishear them.

    isolde drew her sword
    Ysolde drew her sword.

  • It never rewrites you

    Every sentence is checked against what you actually said. If tidying changed a word, you get your own words instead.

    the house was quiet as a held breath
    The house was quiet as a held breath.

It runs on your computer

Speech recognition and post-processing both run on your own machine. There’s no account and no subscription.

An alpha, for now

While beardictate is being tested, it sends what you dictate (the recordings and the text) to its developer, to fix problems and train better post-processing. What it sends.

Works offline

The first time it runs it downloads its speech models, and the post-processing model that tidies your words when you choose one. After that it dictates with no internet; the alpha sends its test data when it’s back online.

No special hardware

It runs on an ordinary CPU and uses your graphics card if you have one, but never needs one.

Free and open source

MIT licensed. Read the code if you want to know exactly what it does.

Always listening, only when you want it to type

Leave the microphone on while you work. Say “bear, wake up” and it starts typing. Say “bear, go to sleep” and it stops, and nothing you say while it’s asleep is typed or kept. A shortcut (Ctrl+` unless you change it) does the same, and the bear in your system tray shows you which it’s doing.

And if a bear in your story is told to go to sleep halfway through a sentence, dictation keeps going.

Bear, wake up. Bear, go to sleep.
